Model & method

The greedy simulation uses the measured PV generation and house load of the day (both independent of the control) and charges without limit: efficiency 0.96, charge cap 5.5 kW, BMS taper from 94 % SOC (linear down to 150 W at 97 %). Discharging: SOC drain = load / η + standby, down to the discharge floor. The discharge parameters are an empirical fit on the reference system (see dashboard/README.md for re-calibration).

Export 11:00–15:00 is the core metric: in this window the grid carries the most PV. The less is exported there, the more of the midday peak the battery absorbed — the greedy world is long full by then and pushes everything into the grid.

Contribution to the neighbourhood peak assumes that neighbouring systems charge greedily and share our weather — then the greedy curve also describes their export profile. Shown is our deviation at the moment of their maximum. It is usually negative because our profile is anti-correlated with a greedily charging neighbourhood: while their batteries absorb the surplus they export nothing and we export more; once they are full and dump everything into the grid, we hold back because we still have capacity. The statement holds against a storing neighbourhood; against systems without storage it would be weaker.

Forecast source shows which rest forecast drove the control that day. Solcast (minute-accurate from the 30-minute raw periods) with automatic fallback to Forecast.Solar × correction factor when Solcast fails or its data is older than ten hours.

Ø all days averages every curve time slot by time slot over all completed days, outage days excluded; the inverter limit then is an average too (share of throttled days at that time). The KPIs are means of the daily values — the Ø export peak is the mean of the daily peaks, not the peak of the averaged curve, which is much lower due to smoothing.

Both worlds are identical by definition until the first real throttling takes effect (inverter limit < 25 A with surplus); the simulation re-couples to the measurements once the SOC difference is ≤ 1 point and the limit is open. The written inverter limit comes from the read-back-verified write log, not from the target sensor.
